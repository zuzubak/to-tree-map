Trees counted against the length of street they stand on, block by block, across km of Toronto streets.

Five classes, cut at quintiles, so each holds about the same number of blocks. The breaks are printed because that makes the class widths uneven — the alternative, equal widths, would put most of the city in one colour to leave room for a handful of extremes.

Distribution

How this is measured

Each line is one centreline segment — one block of one street, between intersections. Every tree in the inventory is snapped to the nearest segment carrying its street name, and the count is divided by the block's own length.

Two kinds of street are left out, both because they are not street-tree frontage. Expressways and their ramps are grade-separated through-routes, and leaving them in would stripe the map with false zeroes along the 401, the DVP and the Gardiner. Laneways are almost never planted — 3,918 of the city's 4,146 laneway segments hold no inventoried tree at all — and left in they were two fifths of every block coloured "no street trees", which says more about what a laneway is than about Toronto's canopy. The few trees genuinely on one are still counted, against whichever real street runs within 60 m.

Blocks shorter than 20 m are left out too — a 6 m connector with two trees is not 33 trees per 100 m of streetscape.

What this cannot tell you

A zero is not proof of a bare street. This is the City's inventory of street trees: trees on private front lawns, in parks and on institutional land are not in it, so a leafy street of deep private gardens can read as empty here.
